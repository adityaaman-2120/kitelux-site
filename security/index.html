Security

Cryptography, architecture and responsible disclosure

Cryptography approach

KiteVault encrypts your vault with AES-256-GCM. The key is derived from your master password with Argon2id. We use vetted, widely reviewed cryptographic libraries and never write our own cryptography.

No-servers architecture

Kitelux runs no servers that receive your vault, passwords or usage. Your vault is stored as a single encrypted file in the app's private storage on your phone, and your master password is never stored and never leaves your phone. Because of this, we cannot see, recover or reset your data or master password.

The only network features are optional: Google Drive backup (an already-encrypted file, sent directly between your phone and your own Google Drive) and the password breach check (only the first 5 characters of a SHA-1 hash are sent). See the KiteVault Privacy Policy for details.

Responsible disclosure

If you believe you have found a security issue in KiteVault or on kitelux.com, please email support@kitelux.com. Please include:

  • A description of the issue and its potential impact.
  • Steps to reproduce it, or a proof of concept.
  • The app version and Android version, or the URL, affected.
  • How you would like to be contacted and, if you wish, how to credit you.

We aim to reply to every report. Please give us reasonable time to investigate and fix an issue before you disclose it publicly, and do not access or modify other people's data.

Our machine-readable contact details are in security.txt.

Out of scope

  • Rooted or malware-infected devices, and physical access to an unlocked phone.
  • Loss of access caused by a forgotten master password without a recovery key.
  • Unencrypted files that you export yourself (for example CSV or PDF).
  • Vulnerabilities in third-party services such as Google or Have I Been Pwned.
  • Social engineering, phishing, spam, and denial-of-service or volumetric testing.
  • Reports from automated scanners with no demonstrated impact.
